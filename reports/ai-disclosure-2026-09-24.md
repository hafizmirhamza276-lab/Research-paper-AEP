## 1. Exactly what IEEE requires

IEEE Author Center, *Submission and Peer Review Policies*, section on the use of
artificial intelligence.

- **URL:** https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/
- **Retrieved:** 2026-09-24

Three obligations, quoted:

1. **Where.** "The use of content generated by artificial intelligence (AI) in an
   article (including but not limited to text, figures, images, and code) shall
   be disclosed in the acknowledgments section".
2. **Which system.** "The AI system used shall be identified".
3. **Which sections, and how much.** "Specific sections of the article that use
   AI-generated content shall be identified and accompanied by a brief
   explanation regarding the level at which the AI system was used".

One carve-out, quoted: using AI for editing and grammar enhancement is standard
practice and "is generally outside the intent of the above policy"; disclosure
for that use is "not required, but recommended".

**Reading it against this paper.** The carve-out does not apply. The assistance
here reached the implementation, the harness, the analysis pipeline, the gates
and the prose — not grammar. So all three obligations bind, and obligation (2) —
identify the system — is the one the anonymous build fails.

## 2. What the named build says

`paper/main.tex:203-220`, rendered in `paper/main.pdf` p. 22-23 under
**ACKNOWLEDGMENT**. Verbatim from the PDF:

> The author used Anthropic's Claude (Claude Code, Opus and Sonnet models) and
> OpenAI's Codex as coding and drafting assistants throughout this work. Their
> use was not confined to one part of it: the protocol implementation, the
> fault-injection harness, the analysis pipeline, the verification gates, the
> supporting documentation and the prose of this manuscript were all produced
> with that assistance, under the author's direction and subject to the author's
> review. The exact model identifiers are not listed here because such a list
> goes stale; they are recorded in the artifact repository's commit trailers and
> in the per-assistant configuration files committed beside them, which are the
> authoritative record. The phase prompts issued from Phase 8 onward are
> committed in that repository, each before the data of the phase it governs;
> Phases 1A–7 carry no such record, and the project's own audit records that gap
> rather than reconstructing it.
>
> No AI system is an author. Every measurement reported here was collected by
> the harness on the author's host and is reproducible from the archived raw
> runs; every number in the manuscript is generated from those results by the
> artifact's table generator and re-derived by its independent number check. The
> author is solely responsible for the content of this article, including all
> claims, analysis and conclusions.

**Assessment.** Obligations (1) and (3) are met and then some. Obligation (2) is
met. The defect is in the third sentence, which makes a **verifiable factual
claim about the repository that is false for one of the two named systems** —
§4 below.

## 3. What the anonymous build says

`paper/main.tex:167-173`, rendered in `paper/main-anon.pdf` under
**ACKNOWLEDGMENT**. Verbatim from the PDF:

> The author used generative AI coding and drafting assistants throughout this
> work (implementation, harness, analysis pipeline, documentation and manuscript
> prose) under the author's direction and review. No AI system is an author; the
> author is solely responsible for the content of this article, including all
> claims, analysis and conclusions.

**Assessment.** Obligations (1) and (3) are met. Obligation (2) — *"The AI system
used shall be identified"* — is **not met**. "Generative AI coding and drafting
assistants" identifies a category, not a system.

The source comment at `main.tex:161-166` states the reasoning:

> IEEE requires the AI disclosure in the acknowledgment of the article
> SUBMITTED. TSE is double-anonymous, so the submitted article is this build,
> and the requirement attaches here too. What is stripped is only what
> identifies: no repository, no host, no script paths, no phase record. What
> survives is the disclosure itself -- which tools, over what scope, under whose
> direction, and who is responsible.

The reasoning is sound and its conclusion is right — the requirement does attach
to the submitted article. What went wrong is that "which tools" was implemented
as a category rather than as names, while IEEE asks for the system.

**Note on the premise.** *"TSE is double-anonymous"* is false — see
`reports/tse-venue-2026-09-24.md`. Under single-anonymous review the *named*
build is the submitted article, which makes the anonymous build's gap moot for
TSE specifically. It is still worth fixing: the anonymous build is the
deliverable for any double-anonymous venue this paper is redirected to, and a
build that fails an IEEE policy should not sit in the repository waiting to be
submitted somewhere.

## 4. Could naming the AI systems in the anonymous build identify the author?

**No.** The author's belief is correct. Four reasons, in descending strength:

1. **The names are not author attributes.** Claude, Claude Code and Codex are
   mass-market commercial products. Naming them places the author in a
   population of millions. De-anonymization requires an attribute that narrows
   the candidate set; a vendor name does not narrow it at all.
2. **The paper already discloses the category and the full scope.** A reviewer
   reading the anonymous build already knows the author used generative AI
   assistants across implementation, harness, analysis, documentation and prose.
   Adding "Claude and Codex" adds no inference about *who* that is that the
   existing sentence did not already license.
3. **IEEE's own guidance treats these as separable.** The society page's
   anonymization obligation is about author identity in files, file properties
   and preliminary versions — it says nothing about tool names, and the AI
   policy demands the system be named in the acknowledgment of the submitted
   article. If naming a system de-anonymized authors, the two policies would be
   in direct conflict for every double-anonymous IEEE venue. They are not.
4. **The genuinely identifying strings are already stripped and stay stripped.**
   `main.tex:159-178` strips the name, contact, ORCID, GitHub URL, archive DOI
   and PDF DocInfo, and `check_paper_numbers.py::check_anonymous_build` plus
   `prove_anonymous_gate.sh` enforce it. Adding two vendor names touches none of
   that machinery.

**The one thing to be careful about.** Do not import the named build's *third*
sentence into the anonymous build. "the artifact repository's commit trailers",
"the per-assistant configuration files committed beside them" and "the phase
prompts issued from Phase 8 onward" point at a repository whose URL the
anonymous build deliberately withholds; and a reviewer who found that repository
from the description would have the author's name. The proposed wording in §6
therefore names the systems and stops.

## 5. What the repository actually records, and what would make the disclosure accurate about Codex

### The claim under test

> "they are recorded in the artifact repository's commit trailers and in the
> per-assistant configuration files committed beside them, which are the
> authoritative record."

### The evidence

Commit trailers, at `44cb5d1` over 495 commits:

```
$ git log --format='%(trailers:key=Co-Authored-By,valueonly)' | grep -v '^$' | sort | uniq -c
    403 Claude Opus 5 (1M context) <noreply@anthropic.com>
```

**One identifier, on 403 of 495 commits. No other identifier appears in any
trailer.**

Per-assistant configuration files — `.claude/` is tracked (12 files):

```
$ grep -rn "^model:" $(git ls-files .claude)
.claude/agents/aep-orchestrator.md:4:model: claude-opus-4-7
.claude/agents/hld-designer.md:4:model: claude-haiku-4-5-20251001
.claude/agents/implementer.md:4:model: claude-sonnet-4-6
.claude/agents/opus-fixer.md:4:model: claude-opus-4-7
.claude/agents/tech-designer.md:4:model: claude-sonnet-4-6
```

So the recorded set is:

| identifier | where recorded |
|---|---|
| `Claude Opus 5 (1M context)` | commit trailers, 403 commits |
| `claude-opus-4-7` | `.claude/agents/aep-orchestrator.md:4`, `.claude/agents/opus-fixer.md:4` |
| `claude-sonnet-4-6` | `.claude/agents/implementer.md:4`, `.claude/agents/tech-designer.md:4` |
| `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` | `.claude/agents/hld-designer.md:4` |
| **any Codex model** | **nowhere** |

Codex is evidenced by name only, in `CODEX_PROMPTS.md` and
`WEEKEND_CODEX_PROMPTS.md`. Neither names a model; the closest either comes is
`CODEX_PROMPTS.md:263`, *"executed by a different model (Codex)"*. No `.codex/`,
`.openai/` or equivalent configuration directory is tracked
(`git ls-files | grep -iE '^\.(codex|openai|agents)'` is empty).

`gpt-5.6-luna` does appear in tracked files — `experiments/harness/azure_client.py:136`,
`experiments/harness/planner.py:64`, the phase-40 prompts and
`tests/test_azure_client.py`. **That is the phase-40 Azure planner deployment**,
the LLM placed in the *caller* position by the agent-reachability experiment. It
is not a drafting assistant and must not be offered as one. Using it to satisfy
this sentence would be a fabrication.

### Two defects, not one

The audit found that the record is incomplete for Codex. Re-checking the whole
sentence turns up a second, in the opposite direction:

1. **The claim is false for Codex.** No identifier is recorded anywhere, so the
   trailers and the configuration files are not "the authoritative record" for
   it; there is no record.
2. **The named build under-reports Claude.** It says *"Claude Code, Opus and
   Sonnet models"*. The tracked configuration also records
   `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, and the trailers record a fourth family member,
   `Claude Opus 5 (1M context)`, which is not the same model as
   `claude-opus-4-7`. Haiku is named nowhere in the disclosure.

### What the disclosure would have to say to be accurate

Given that `.claude/` records Claude models and nothing records a Codex model
identifier, there are exactly **two** honest options. The project cannot
reconstruct a Codex identifier after the fact — doing so would be writing a
number it does not have, which is the practice `main.tex:8-25` exists to forbid.

- **Option A — narrow the provenance claim to Claude, and say plainly that Codex
  has no recorded identifier.** Keeps both systems named, as IEEE requires, and
  tells the truth about the asymmetry. This is the recommended option and is
  what §6 implements.
- **Option B — drop the identifier sentence entirely** and let "Claude and
  Codex" stand alone. IEEE requires the system be identified; it does not
  require exact model identifiers. This is compliant and shorter, and it loses
  the artifact's best claim about its own provenance. Reasonable if §9's word
  budget is the binding constraint (`reports/tse-venue-2026-09-24.md` §2).

A third option — **drop Codex from the disclosure** — is available only if Codex
was in fact used for nothing that reached the article. `CODEX_PROMPTS.md` and
`WEEKEND_CODEX_PROMPTS.md` describe an adversarial audit and a TLA+/Hypothesis
task, both of which reached the artifact, so on the evidence in the repository
**this option is not available**. Recorded here so it is visibly ruled out
rather than silently skipped.

## 6. The exact replacement wording

Two edits, both in `paper/main.tex`. **Neither is applied.**

### 6.1 The anonymous branch — replaces `\aidisclosure` at `main.tex:167-173`

> The author used Anthropic's Claude (Claude Code, with Opus, Sonnet and Haiku
> models) and OpenAI's Codex as generative-AI coding and drafting assistants
> throughout this work. Their use was not confined to one part of it: the
> protocol implementation, the fault-injection harness, the analysis pipeline,
> the verification gates, the supporting documentation and the prose of this
> manuscript were all produced with that assistance, under the author's
> direction and subject to the author's review. No AI system is an author; the
> author is solely responsible for the content of this article, including all
> claims, analysis and conclusions.

What changed and why:

- **The systems are named.** This is the S7 fix and the IEEE obligation (2).
- **The scope list is promoted from a parenthesis to the named build's sentence**,
  which is a fuller statement of obligation (3) at a cost of about fifteen words.
- **Nothing identifying is added.** No repository, no URL, no trailers, no phase
  record, no host. The strings this branch strips stay stripped, so
  `check_anonymous_build` and `prove_anonymous_gate.sh` are unaffected.

### 6.2 The named branch — replaces the third sentence of `\aidisclosure` at `main.tex:211-215`

Current:

> The exact model identifiers are not listed here because such a list goes
> stale; they are recorded in the artifact repository's commit trailers and in
> the per-assistant configuration files committed beside them, which are the
> authoritative record.

Replacement:

> The exact model identifiers are not listed here because such a list goes
> stale. For Claude they are recorded in the artifact repository: the commit
> trailers name the model that signed each commit, and the per-assistant
> configuration files committed beside them name the model each subagent ran.
> No model identifier was recorded for Codex at the time of use and the project
> does not reconstruct one after the fact, so its use is evidenced by the
> committed prompt files rather than by an identifier.

And, in the **first** sentence, `(Claude Code, Opus and Sonnet models)` becomes
`(Claude Code, with Opus, Sonnet and Haiku models)`.

What changed and why:

- **"which are the authoritative record" is gone.** It was the false half: those
  files are authoritative for Claude and silent on Codex.
- **The Codex gap is disclosed rather than covered.** A reviewer who greps for a
  Codex identifier now finds the paper already told them there isn't one.
- **Haiku is added**, because `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` is recorded at
  `.claude/agents/hld-designer.md:4` and the sentence claims to enumerate.
- **Everything after it is unchanged** — the phase-prompt sentence, the
  Phases 1A–7 gap, and the second paragraph.

### 6.3 What applying this costs

Both are `\newcommand` bodies in `paper/main.tex`, so applying them requires all
four builds to be rerun (`build_paper.sh` treats `main.tex` as a source for the
supplementary staleness check) and the page counts rechecked. The anonymous
branch's replacement is about 25 words longer than the current one and the named
branch's is about 20 words longer; neither is near a page boundary, but the
named build is at 24 pages against a 12-page budget, so this is the wrong
direction and should ride along with the length pass rather than ahead of it.
