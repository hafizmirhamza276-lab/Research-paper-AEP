## 1. What was wrong

`experiments/run_matrix.py:1316-1324` injects `timeout_probability: 0.15` and
`server_error_probability: 0.05`, and line 1197 applies them at the single
`fault_overrides` call site that every plan entry passes through. §6.1 said the

## 2. The three macros, and why they are generated

`scripts/paper_tables.py` now parses `FAULTS` out of `experiments/run_matrix.py`
with `ast` and emits:

| macro | value | provenance comment |
|---|---|---|
| `\ProviderTimeoutPct` | 15 | `experiments/run_matrix.py \| FAULTS['timeout_probability'] as a percentage` |
| `\ProviderErrorPct` | 5 | same, `server_error_probability` |
| `\ProviderDuplicatePct` | 0 | same, `duplicate_response_probability` |

Each carries a second provenance line: *applied at the single
`fault_overrides` call site, so every regime carries it.*

Parsed rather than imported, because `experiments.run_matrix` pulls the harness
and its Redis client in at module scope and the generator reads results, never
protocol state. Generated rather than typed, for the reason
`\BootstrapResamples` is generated: a constant living in the code can move
without the sentence quoting it moving.

Three more come from the crash-free collection, which is the comparison §6.6
now needs:

| macro | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| `\CrashFreeDupLow` | 0.1333 | `ws5-2026-09-10/t1-p0-everysec/analysis/per-cell-metrics.csv`, `metric=undetected_duplicate_rate regime=p0`, lowest of B0/B1/B2/B4 |
| `\CrashFreeDupHigh` | 0.1800 | same, highest |
| `\CrashFreeDupExec` | 150 | executions per system behind both |

`per-cell-metrics.csv` is this generator's only rate source, so the new rates
come from it and not from `per-execution.csv`.

Every other generated file is byte-identical after the change. The five tables
regenerate `IDENTICAL`; `numbers.tex` differs by the six additions and the three
removals below.

## 3. What changed in the manuscript

### 3.1 §6.1, the disclosure

The `Faults` paragraph now opens on the provider and says the configuration
reaches every run:

> Two fault sources run in every cell, and the first is not a regime. The
> provider times out on \ProviderTimeoutPct\% of requests and returns a 5xx on
> \ProviderErrorPct\%, answering the rest after a constant delay. It never
> returns a duplicate of its own (\ProviderDuplicatePct\%), so every duplicate
> the oracle records was applied by a caller that called twice. One code path in
> the runner applies this configuration to every run, which means it holds in
> every regime below, including the crash-free one.

The `p0` item no longer says *"nothing is injected"*:

> **crash-free** (`p0`): no worker is killed and the store is left alone. The
> provider faults above still apply.

And a new paragraph says what it means for reading the rates: the four retrying
baselines duplicate at `\CrashFreeDupLow`--`\CrashFreeDupHigh` over
`\CrashFreeDupExec` executions each with no crash at all, where AEP-full, B3 and
B4b record none; every pooled baseline rate sits on that background; a low
per-cell rate is evidence about the timeouts and not about the crash.

### 3.2 §3 and §5 were checked and need nothing

- **§3** is the protocol's fault model, F1–F5, which is a statement about what
  the protocol is designed against. The injection rates are a property of the
  evaluation, not of the model, and F4 already names *"delayed or duplicated
  external responses"*. Putting an evaluation setting in §3 would be a category
  error.
- **§5** mentions the mock provider only in a line count.

§6.1 is the only home the disclosure has, which is why §9's partial disclosure
now points at it.

### 3.3 §9.1's partial disclosure now points at §6.1

`08-threats.tex:82-84` said the endpoint has *"a configurable delay, timeout and
error distribution"*, which named the surface without a rate. It now reads
*"run at the delay, timeout and error rates \cref{sec:eval-setup} states"*.
Shorter, and it stops standing alone.

### 3.4 §6.6, re-attributed

**Removed.** The claim that the duplicates follow from post-crash re-execution,
with its three per-cell rates, and the sentence *"The systems that hold a
pre-dispatch record, or that decline to retry, reach it at none"*, which is
false for B4: B4 holds a durable pre-dispatch record (§6.3.1, and Trace 3 in §2)
and duplicated at 0.2000 in the same paragraph.

**Kept**, because it is what the cell settles and what B2 leans on:
`\AbdZeroDispatchApplied` = 0 of 450 executions with an applied effect and no
dispatch attempt, against 82 of B4b's 90 in the deferred cell; B4b's zero
applied over zero dispatches in all three of its cells; AEP-full's and B3's
matching zeros.

**Added**, stating the background rather than leaving it to be inferred:

> The duplicates in this cell are the provider's timeouts and not the
> re-execution, and we draw no duplicate-rate conclusion from it. A kill before
> transmission leaves one re-execution, and one re-execution applies one effect;
> every duplicate here required a second dispatch inside the same execution,
> which is what a timeout produces. The rates are \AbdDupRange per cell, against
> \CrashFreeDupLow--\CrashFreeDupHigh for the same systems in the crash-free
> cells, where no worker is killed at all.

The explanation of why effects appeared at all is kept and corrected: *"a
supervisor re-executes, and the four systems configured to retry call again"*.
The old wording attributed the second call to the absence of a durable record,
which is wrong for B4; B4 calls again because its retry policy says so.

### 3.5 The engine comparison: stated once, in §6.2

**Decision: state it once, labelled, rather than drop it.** Recommended and
applied.

The engine run is the paper's only third-party system, and §9.2 already concedes
that everything else is *"two systems we wrote, measured by a harness we wrote,
against a provider we wrote"*. Dropping it makes that weaker, and it discards a
collection that took three attempts. What survives re-attribution is also the
part worth keeping: each configuration lands in the corner the model assigns it,
with zero events of the other kind, and neither declared ambiguity in any run.
That is a statement about which corner of the trilemma a durable-execution
engine reaches, and it does not require the fault to be a crash. An engine that
duplicates at 0.1576 under timeouts is still an engine that duplicates silently.

What does not survive is the magnitude comparison against B4 and B4b. Removed
from all four places:

| where | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| §6.2 (`06-evaluation.tex:204`) | *"the magnitudes are closer than an earlier comparison of ours suggested"*, then B4's phase-53 rates against `\BfiveDup` and B4b's *"loses nothing at all"* against `\BfivebLost` | the single labelled statement: the provider produced those events, not the crash; the cell kills one worker per ten-execution run and `\BfiveDup` sits at the provider's `\ProviderTimeoutPct`\% rate; the result is about an ambiguous call and not about crash recovery; we do not set its rates against B4's or B4b's |
| §6.2 (`:234`) | *"The separation should therefore be read from the point estimates, which differ by roughly 0.8"* | deleted. It quoted the old deferred-kill comparison |
| §6.2 (`:244`) | *"The shape of the argument transfers and the magnitude is our model's"* | deleted as a restatement; *"a gap of that size"* became *"a large gap between the two"*, which is what was pre-registered |
| §7.2 (`07-related.tex:208`) | *"The rates were several times below B4's and B4b's at the same crash point"*, plus a restatement of the bounds | the corner result and a pointer: *"\cref{sec:eval-rq1} reports the rates, what produced them and the four things that bound them; we do not restate them here"* |

The exposure paragraph that followed the old §6.2 comparison was merged into the
single statement, since it said the same thing twice.

### 3.6 Figure 2's caption

The caption claimed *"any interruption after the request is on the wire is
enough"*, while the figure's own `before_intent_write` bars show 0.122–0.244
from an interruption before anything was sent. That is the B1 misreading in a
caption, so it is in this fix:

> The baselines' rate is near one wherever the kill lands once the request is on
> the wire, which is why a mechanism acting only *after* the call cannot help. At
> `before_intent_write`, where nothing has been sent, it falls back to the
> timeout background of \cref{sec:eval-setup}.

The figure's **staleness** is F1 and is not fixed here. It is entry 8 in
`claims-to-review.md`.

## 4. The sweep: everything else the background could explain

Each outcome the manuscript attributes to a crash, recomputed against the
crash-free cells, which carry the same injected faults and no worker kill.

| claim | crashed | crash-free | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| baselines' undetected duplicates, pooled | 0.7417–0.7833 (B0–B2), 0.4467–0.5067 (B4) | 0.1333–0.1800 | **holds.** The background is a floor under it, and §6.1 now says so |
| B4b loses the effect where B4 duplicates | 0.4600 / 0.4267 / 0.4467 per class | 0.1467 | **holds.** Fisher $p = 4.2\times10^{-9}$, $1.1\times10^{-7}$, $1.6\times10^{-8}$. The crashed number contains the background but is not produced by it |
| AEP-full and B3 record zero duplicates and zero lost effects | 0/450 each | 0/150 each | **unaffected.** Zero in both |
| prevention, `redis-kill-preack` | AEP-full 10/30 applied, B3 28/30 | not applicable | **unaffected.** Every execution in that cell applied 0 or 1 effect, so no timeout retry is inside those counts |
| baselines' lost effects | 0.0056–0.0139 | 0.0000–0.0067 | not a claim the paper leans on; both are near zero |
| declared ambiguity, AEP-full and B3 | 193/540 and 195/540 | **0/150 each** | **holds on `AUTHORITATIVE_READBACK` only.** See below |

**The one gap, and it is recorded rather than patched.** The crash-free cells
are `payments`, which is `AUTHORITATIVE_READBACK`. On that class a read-back
settles a timed-out call, so AEP-full declares no ambiguity there without a
crash. On `POSITIVE_ONLY_READBACK` and `NO_READBACK` no crash-free cell exists,
and on those classes a read-back cannot settle a timed-out call, so a timeout
alone should produce a declared ambiguity. §6.2's residual-by-capability result
is where that would matter. It is `claims-to-review.md` entry 7, with the cell
that would close it: `p0` for AEP-full and B3 on the two other endpoints, three
runs each, machine time only.

`06-evaluation.tex:245-246`, the *"crash point at which no effect can possibly
exist"* sentence that phase 53's report flagged, no longer exists. Nothing else
in the manuscript attributes an outcome to crash recovery that the background
could explain.
