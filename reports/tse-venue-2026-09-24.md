## 1. The review model

### What the source says

IEEE Computer Society, *How to Get Published: Author Guidelines for IEEE Computer
Society Publications*.

- **URL:** https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources
- **Retrieved:** 2026-09-24

On double-anonymous review:

> "The peer review process assures the anonymity of the reviewers. You can
> request a review in which your identity is also kept from the reviewers."

and, immediately qualifying it:

> "IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing, IEEE Transactions on Computers, **IEEE
> Transactions on Software Engineering**, IEEE Transactions on Dependable and
> Secure Computing, and IEEE Transactions on Emerging Topics in Computing do not
> offer this option."

The same page states the author's obligation where the option *is* available:

> "all electronic files (including, but not limited to, supplemental material and
> preliminary/conference versions), file properties, and materials submitted for
> double-anonymous review do not reveal your identity or the identities of
> contributing authors."

### Verdict

**The audit is right.** TSE does not offer double-anonymous review. A TSE
submission is single-anonymous: reviewer identities are withheld from authors,
author identities are not withheld from reviewers, and author names and
affiliations belong in the submitted manuscript.

### What follows for this project

1. **`paper/main-anon.pdf` and `paper/supplementary-anon.pdf` are the wrong
   deliverable for TSE.** They are not defective — they are built for a review
   model this journal does not run.
2. **The submitted article is the named build.** IEEE's generative-AI disclosure
   requirement attaches to the article submitted, so it attaches to the named
   build, which already names the AI systems. The comment at
   `paper/main.tex:161-166` reasons the other way — *"TSE is double-anonymous, so
   the submitted article is this build"* — from the premise this report refutes.
   See `reports/ai-disclosure-2026-09-24.md`; the anonymous build's disclosure
   gap remains worth fixing anyway, for the reason given there.
3. **The anonymity machinery does not have to be removed.** It can simply go
   unused. `prove_anonymous_gate.sh` and the anonymous builds cost nothing to
   keep and are the right deliverable for a venue that does run
   double-anonymous review, which is a live possibility if TSE declines.
4. **`reports/camera-ready-checklist.md` inherits the error.** Its §7.5 framing —
   *"Things invisible to a double-anonymous reviewer that must be fixed before
   camera-ready"* — is wrong about visibility: under single-anonymous review the
   author identity, ORCID and artifact URLs are visible to reviewers from the
   start. The items still need fixing; the reason they were deferred does not
   hold. **Not changed by this session.**

## 2. The page limit

### What the sources say

**Source A** — the same IEEE Computer Society author-guidelines page
(https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources, retrieved 2026-09-24):

> "12 formatted pages for Transactions and four formatted pages for Letters,
> including references and author biographies."

> "Any pages or fraction thereof exceeding this limit are charged $220 per page."

> "Authors who do not pay Mandatory APCs without a waiver shall be barred from
> submitting future articles to IEEE publications until such time as their
> previous outstanding charges are paid."

**Source B** — *2026 IEEE Publications Article Processing Charge (APC) List*,
IEEE Author Center.

- **URL:** https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/IEEE-Article-Processing-Charges-List.pdf
- **Retrieved:** 2026-09-24
- Document title as printed: *"2026 IEEE Publications Article Processing Charge
  (APC) List"*
- Column group as printed: *"Number of Pages Allowed Without Incurring Overlength
  Charges Per Manuscript Type"*, with a **Regular** column.

The **TSE** row reads:

| Acronym | OA Pub Type | OA Fee | Overlength Charges | Regular pages allowed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSE | Hybrid | $2,800 | **$220** per page | **12** |

Two independent sources, same numbers.

### What happens past it

**Not an automatic desk return.** The charge is a **Mandatory Overlength Page
Charge (MOPC)**, assessed under IEEE Policy 6.10 on *accepted* papers, computed
on the **final formatted** article after editing and layout — not on the
submission PDF. It is not negotiable, and non-payment bars future IEEE
submissions (Source A, quoted above).

The audit's §3 item 7 says *"At 24 pages … length could trigger a desk return."*
That is a plausible editorial risk, but it is **not** the documented mechanism;
the documented mechanism is a bill. Both should be stated, and the audit's
framing should be narrowed to "editor's discretion" rather than policy.

### The arithmetic, as an estimate

`paper/main.pdf` is 24 pages in IEEEtran compsoc. If the published layout came
out at the same length, the overlength would be 12 pages at $220 = **$2,640**,
excluding any open-access APC. **This is an estimate and should not be quoted as
a figure**: MOPC is computed on the final formatted article, which is not the
submission PDF, and the supplementary (7 pages) is supplemental material rather
than article pages.

The practical reading: the manuscript is **double** the length the journal
budgets for a regular paper. The audit's §5.1 length finding and the
`PAPER_ROADMAP.md` *DEFERRED TO THE LENGTH PASS* items are not polish; on this
evidence they are the difference between a 12-page paper and a $2,640 invoice,
and the ~3,700 words of §9 that restate §6 are the first place to look.

## 3. What I could not retrieve, and why

**The TSE journal's own author-information page.** The Computer Society Digital
Library pages for TSE are client-rendered; fetching them returns the site header
and nothing else. Attempted and empty:

- `https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/ts/write-for-us/15090` — linked from the
  TSE Call for Papers as *"Submission Guidelines"*
- `https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/ts/write-for-us/15083`
- `https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/ts/about/14409` — linked as
  *"TSE Information"*
- `https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/aboutJournal.jsp?punumber=32` — returned
  HTTP 418

So **this report does not quote the journal's own page.** It quotes the
publisher's society-wide author guidance, which names TSE explicitly, and the
publisher's own 2026 charge list, which carries a TSE row. Both are
authoritative for the two facts at issue, and they agree. The remaining gap is
that neither is TSE's own page, and a JS-capable browser should confirm both
against `/csdl/journal/ts/write-for-us/15090` before submission.

**One contradicting secondary reading, and why I discount it.** IEEE Xplore's
journal-description boilerplate for TSE says articles are reviewed *"using a
single and/or double anonymous peer review process"*. That sentence is the same
generic string Xplore prints across IEEE periodicals, and the same passage
defines the model as one in which *"reviewer identities are not known to authors,
but reviewers know author identities"* — i.e. single-anonymous. It does not
establish that TSE offers double-anonymous review as an author-selectable option,
and it is contradicted by the society guidance that names TSE in a list of
journals that do not. **Anyone rechecking this should start there**, because it
is the only source I found that could be read the other way.
