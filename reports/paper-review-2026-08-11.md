# STAGE 1 — BLIND REVIEW

## R1. Summary (reviewer's own words)

An autonomous agent calls a legacy API that is non-idempotent, offers no idempotency key, and in the worst case cannot even be asked afterwards whether a mutation happened. If the agent crashes around that call, the paper argues, every strategy loses one of three things: retrying risks a silent duplicate, not retrying risks a silent lost effect, and the only remaining option is to *stop and durably declare that the outcome is unknown*. AEP is a protocol built to reach that third corner: a WAITAOF-acknowledged write-ahead intent is a checked precondition of dispatch; state writes are fenced by lease-token + expected-version CAS inside one Lua script; unresolvable outcomes terminate in a declared-ambiguous state that only an operator can exit.

The evaluation (432 runs, 3 780 executions, 126 cells; real SIGKILL at six crash points; three endpoint reconciliation capabilities; five baselines plus one in two configurations) supports two separated claims. First, *detection* — zero undetected duplicates and zero lost effects, with a declared-ambiguity residual whose rate tracks the endpoint's capability — is produced by the durable pre-dispatch record plus the no-re-entry state machine, **not** by the fsync barrier: the barrier ablation (B3) is statistically indistinguishable from AEP-full on every detection metric. Second, *prevention* — withholding the external call when durability cannot be confirmed — is what the barrier buys, visible only under a hard Redis kill (18 real effects withheld, 0.3333 vs 0.9333) and priced at ≈2 s/step under `appendfsync everysec` but ≈15 ms under `always`. The central contribution is therefore stateable in three sentences, which speaks well of the writing: (1) against an uncooperative endpoint the correct target is declared, bounded ambiguity, not exactly-once; (2) the detection half of the write-ahead pattern is nearly free and does not need fsync; (3) the prevention half is what fsync buys, and it is a deployment choice.

## R2. Novelty and related work

The reviewer ran twelve distinct literature searches: (1) exactly-once execution semantics for non-idempotent external calls; (2) transactional outbox / write-ahead intent patterns; (3) LLM-agent tool-call reliability under injected failures, 2025–2026; (4) Olive / "locks with intent"; (5) Restate and modern durable-execution engines; (6) the IETF Idempotency-Key header draft; (7) crash-consistency fault-injection tooling (ALICE, CrashMonkey, Torturing Databases); (8) "declared ambiguity" / fail-closed escalation in agent control planes; (9) agent checkpoint-restore and crash recovery (LogAct, Resume-contract work, Ding et al.); (10) academic treatments of Temporal-style durable execution; (11) TOST equivalence testing; (12) at-most-once/at-least-once delivery-guarantee framing. Findings, ordered by how much they can hurt the paper.

### R2.1 Missed prior work that a reviewer will quote back

**(a) Olive — Setty, Su, Lorch, Zhou, Chen, Patel, Ren, "Realizing the Fault-Tolerance Promise of Cloud Storage Using Locks with Intent," OSDI 2016. Not cited. This is the single most damaging omission.** Olive's core abstraction is *literally named* "locks with intent": a durable intent record written to the coordination store, combined with locks, giving exactly-once execution of a code snippet across client crashes and concurrent duplicate executions, with the intent record as the write-ahead precondition. The manuscript's C2 ("a durably acknowledged write-ahead intent is a checked precondition of dispatch authority") reads, to anyone who knows Olive, as a restatement of Olive's mechanism with a stronger durability barrier and a weaker environment. The paper cites Beldi [19], and Beldi's own §3.1 opens with "Initial inspiration: Olive" — so the omission is not defensible as obscurity; it is one hop from a work the paper already cites. AEP's genuine delta over Olive exists: Olive's exactly-once holds for steps whose effects live inside the storage's atomicity scope (or are made recoverable), whereas AEP's premise is an external effect that is *outside* any atomicity scope and *unqueryable*, and AEP's answer is a terminal declared-ambiguous outcome Olive does not have. But the paper must say this itself; at present a reviewer discovers Olive, finds "locks with intent" in the title, and the novelty of C2 collapses until the rebuttal. **Verdict: the delta is real but is currently neither stated nor defended in the paper.**

**(b) The 2026 agent-reliability wave, three papers, none cited.** The paper's §VII(e) says "The reliability literature for this setting is young" and cites only ACRFence [24]. The reviewer's searches found at least three closer 2026 works:

- **arXiv 2608.02645, "Verified Tool Calls Improve LLM Agent Reliability Under Non-Atomic Failures" (31 Jul 2026).** Same problem statement almost verbatim — tool calls are non-atomic; timeouts after dispatch; duplicate actions — and a wrapper with postcondition verification, verify-before-retry, and idempotency keys, evaluated under injected non-atomic failures. It differs from AEP in assuming an endpoint that *can* be verified (AEP's AUTH/POS-ONLY classes) and in offering no answer for NO-READBACK; it also measures in simulation rather than under real SIGKILL. That difference is exactly the comparison the related-work section should be making, and it currently cannot because the paper predates or missed the work. Given the submission date, this may be contemporaneous rather than prior; either way TSE reviewers in late 2026 will know it.
- **arXiv 2604.07988, "LogAct: Enabling Agentic Reliability via Shared Logs" (Apr 2026).** Log-before-acting for agents, positioned explicitly against durable-workflow engines on the grounds that agents' control flow is open-ended and "no predefined undo exists" — the same premise-removal move AEP makes against sagas. Direct conceptual competitor for C1's framing.
- **arXiv 2606.20520, "Sovereign Execution Broker" (2026).** A broker for agentic control planes whose crash-recovery rule is: once a certificate is consumed, retry is forbidden *unless a durable pre-invocation ledger state, an idempotency token, or target-side evidence proves no mutation was attempted* — i.e., a fail-closed pre-dispatch ledger with evidence-classed reconciliation, overlapping both C2 and the Table II capability taxonomy.

None of these has AEP's measured trilemma, its ablation, or its fault-injection depth — the *evaluation* remains clearly ahead of all three — but the claim in §VII(e) that ACRFence is "the nearest work" is no longer true, and the introduction's implication that the third corner is unoccupied needs qualification.

**(c) The fault-injection methodology has an uncited ancestry.** SIGKILL at named instruction boundaries, an independent ground-truth oracle, and a dm-flakey `drop_writes` device are all methodologically descended from the crash-consistency testing literature — Pillai et al.'s ALICE (OSDI 2014), Zheng et al.'s "Torturing Databases for Fun and Profit" (OSDI 2014), Mohan et al.'s CrashMonkey/ACE (OSDI 2018 / TOS 2019). `refs.bib` contains none of them (checked by grep). This does not threaten novelty — the paper's fault harness is applied, not claimed as a contribution — but a TSE reviewer from the storage community will expect the lineage acknowledged in §VI or §VII, and the dm-flakey probe in particular should cite the practice it borrows.

**(d) The transactional outbox and the Idempotency-Key draft.** The paper's B3 (intent, no barrier) is, structurally, a single-writer transactional outbox whose relay refuses to re-dispatch; practitioners will map it to that pattern instantly (e.g., microservices.io's outbox page states the relay "might publish a message more than once," which is exactly the failure AEP's no-re-entry edge removes). The IETF `draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header` (active WG document, -07, Oct 2025) is the standardization of the cooperation AEP's premise removes; citing it would sharpen the premise ("the endpoints agents are pointed at do not implement this") from assertion to referenced fact. Neither is cited. Low cost, real credibility gain.

### R2.2 What is fairly characterized

The works the paper does cite were verified against DBLP-indexed metadata during this review and are accurately described: Beldi (OSDI 2020), Boki (SOSP 2021), ExoFlow (OSDI 2023), Durable Functions semantics (PACMPL/OOPSLA 2021), Sagas (SIGMOD 1987), Helland 2007/2012, FLP 1985, Chandra–Toueg 1996, ARIES, Kung–Robinson, Raft — all real, correct venues, correctly summarized. Table XII's claim that Beldi/Boki/ExoFlow/Durable Functions require endpoint cooperation matches those papers' own statements (ExoFlow is explicit that each step must be idempotent, transactional, or compensable). The Temporal quotations in §II and the B4 defaults are sourced to named documentation pages with retrieval dates, and `experiments/baselines/B4_SEMANTICS.md` is an unusually careful fairness argument for a hand-written stand-in — see R3.2. ACRFence (arXiv 2603.20625) exists and is fairly characterized as adjacent (checkpoint-restore semantic rollback), not competing.

### R2.3 Novelty verdict

The delta the paper actually has — a *measured decomposition* of the write-ahead pattern into a detection half (record + no-re-entry, nearly free) and a prevention half (fsync barrier, expensive, visible only under storage-loss faults), plus a terminal declared-ambiguity outcome evaluated as a first-class metric — is (a) clearly stated in §VI-C/§VIII and (b) true as far as this reviewer can determine; no found work performs that ablation or prices the two halves separately. The paper even pre-empts the harshest reading in §VIII ("our most novel mechanism serves the claim with the weakest evidence... the claim with the strongest evidence rests on write-ahead logging, which is decades old") — a candor that will buy goodwill. But C2's mechanism novelty is overstated *by omission* while Olive is uncited, and §VII(e)'s "nearest work" sentence is out of date. Both are fixable in a revision without touching a single experiment.

## R3. Method and validity

### R3.1 Fault model

Six SIGKILL points around the external call (Table III) is the right primary fault model *for the detection claims*: the paper's own Figure 3 shows the baselines' duplication is spread across post-dispatch crash points, which is precisely what the model must cover. Redis hard-kill and dm-flakey write loss extend it to the prevention and durability claims. What the model omits — network partitions between worker and provider (as distinct from worker death), delayed/duplicated provider responses actually *injected* rather than modeled, clock skew, concurrent clients contending for one execution, Redis AOF rewind — is in each case either declared out of scope in Table IV/§III-D or acknowledged in §VIII. Two gaps deserve more than a listing: **(i) F4 (delayed/duplicated external responses) is stated as handled ("recovery waits out a reconciliation delay") but no cell injects a late response arriving *after* recovery has classified the intent** — the one fault that could turn a `confirmed` into a lie — and **(ii) concurrency is absent**: every cell is one worker per execution, so the fenced-CAS machinery of P1, a large fraction of the implementation, is never exercised by the evaluation under contention (the paper's F5 mentions GC-stall handling but no cell runs two live claimants). The paper should either add a small contention regime or state plainly that P1 is tested by the unit suite only.

### R3.2 Baseline fairness

B0/B1/B2 are deliberately weak and the paper says so; they are controls, not competitors, and Figure 3's point (B1/B2 ≈ B0) is a legitimate use of them. The strawman risk concentrates in B4/B4b, and the artifact meets it better than the paper does: `experiments/baselines/B4_SEMANTICS.md` pins every load-bearing behavior (timeout-inferred crash detection, unlimited default Maximum Attempts, at-most-once under Attempts=1) to a quoted, dated Temporal documentation page, and §VIII correctly confines the claim to the *documented default policy*, disclaiming anything about Temporal as a product. Two residual objections a reviewer can still raise: **(i) B4's history and AEP's intent share the same Redis and the same WAITAOF barrier, so B4 inherits AEP's storage stack rather than a real engine's replicated one — fine for the duplicate-semantics claim, but it means Table X's B4 latency row (4015.7 ms) is an artifact of this stack and should not be in a table a hurried reader will screenshot**; the footnote in §VIII says this, the table itself does not. **(ii) The configuration space between the two corners (Attempts=3, heartbeats, schedule-to-close timeouts) is acknowledged as "landing between our two points" but never measured** — acceptable, since the two corners bracket the trade, but the deployment-choice narrative would be stronger with one intermediate point.

### R3.3 Statistics — recomputed

All recomputations below were run against `experiments/results/matrix/analysis/*.csv` in this clone; `make reproduce-figures` separately confirmed the committed tables and macros are byte-identical to a fresh regeneration.

**Recomputation 1 — B4 duplicate rate on AUTH (§VI-B, Table VI: 0.5278).** Pooling the six crashed-regime cells for `B4_DURABLE_WORKFLOW × AUTHORITATIVE_READBACK`: 28/30 + 1/30 + 5/30 + 29/30 + 7/30 + 25/30 = 95/180 = **0.52778**. Matches. The per-crash-point spread (1/30 at `after_intent_before_barrier` vs 29/30 at `after_response_before_resolution`) also confirms the caption's warning that a partial cell would have been unrepresentative.

**Recomputation 2 — the baseline range and headline p-value (§VI-B: "0.77–0.83... significant at p=2.1×10⁻¹⁸³").** Crashed-regime per-capability rates for B0–B2 recompute to 0.7733–0.8267, matching "0.77–0.83". The p-value, however, does **not** come from crashed-regime counts. `comparisons-vs-aep-full.csv` shows the test was run on B0 = 362/480 vs AEP = 0/600, and those denominators decompose as 480 = 450 crashed + 30 crash-free (p0), 600 = 540 crashed + 30 p0 + 30 redis-kill-preack. **The headline significance test pools fault regimes — the exact practice §VI-A(e) bans ("no pooled table. A rate computed across fault regimes is a property of how many runs of each kind were collected") and the practice `ARTIFACT.md` says got `table-1.csv` "banned as a source."** The reviewer re-ran Fisher's exact on crashed-only counts (359/450 vs 0/540): p = 1.15×10⁻¹⁸³ — the conclusion is untouched, the inconsistency is not. The same pooling inflates the ablation's advertised n: "600 executions per arm" is 540 crashed + 30 p0 + 30 redis-kill, and the redis-kill cell records declared-ambiguity 30/30 in *both* arms *by design*, so including it mechanically shrinks the apparent between-arm difference the sentence "declared ambiguity differs by 2 executions in 600 (p=0.95)" reports. Crashed-only: 193/540 vs 195/540 — still differs by 2, Fisher p = 0.949, so again the finding survives; but the paper is quoting a number produced by the method it forbids itself, and a careful reviewer will find it exactly the way this one did.

**Recomputation 3 — the zero-bound (§VI-C1: "The one-sided 95% Wilson limit on a zero numerator over 600 executions is 0.64%").** The Wilson upper bound at 0/600 with z = 1.6449 (one-sided 95%) is **0.449%**; with z = 1.96 it is **0.636%**. The paper's 0.64% is therefore the upper endpoint of a *two-sided* 95% Wilson interval — equivalently a one-sided **97.5%** limit — mislabeled as one-sided 95%. The error is conservative (the quoted bound is looser than the label promises), so no conclusion weakens; but in the paragraph whose entire job is statistical care, the label must match the arithmetic. Fix: either say "97.5% one-sided (the upper end of the two-sided 95% interval)" and keep 0.64, or keep "one-sided 95%" and print 0.45. Additionally, the follow-on sentence "the difference between the two systems is below it as well" is a deterministic consequence of both individual bounds holding, but the *joint* coverage of two simultaneous 95% bounds is ≥90% (Bonferroni), not 95% — one clause fixes it.

**Recomputation 4 — the prevention cell (§VI-C2: 10/30 vs 28/30, p=1.9×10⁻⁶).** Fisher's exact two-tailed on [[10,20],[28,2]] = 1.908×10⁻⁶. Matches. n=30 per arm is small but the effect is enormous; the fragility here is scope (one crash point, one capability class, host-dependent effect size), which §VIII discloses at length, not power.

**Is "bounded below X%" the right statement, or should the ablation be an equivalence test?** For the two *zero-count* metrics (duplicates, lost effects), the paper's choice is correct and its reasoning ("a test between two zero counts has no power... what two zeros do support is a bound") is exactly right; a TOST on 0/540 vs 0/540 adds nothing a Wilson/Clopper-Pearson bound doesn't. Where an equivalence test *is* the right instrument and is missing is **declared ambiguity**, which is non-zero in both arms: the paper reports only a powerless Fisher p=0.95. The reviewer ran the TOST-style 90% CI on the pooled difference (0.375 − 0.3717 = +0.33 pp): **[−4.3 pp, +4.9 pp]**, so equivalence within a ±5 pp margin holds at the 95% level — a strictly stronger and more honest statement than "p=0.95", and it costs one sentence. Recommend adding it (on crashed-only counts).

**Cluster structure.** Rates pool executions across 3 runs/cell; executions within a run share a process, host state, and seed, so they are not independent. The paper handles this correctly where it matters — per-cell CIs are cluster-aware bootstrap, and Fig. 2's caption explicitly tells the reader the bootstrap intervals "are the ones to quote" over the Wilson bars — but the Fisher tests treat executions as independent, which with 3 clusters/cell overstates precision. At effect sizes of 75+ pp against zero this cannot change any verdict; at the 2-execution ambiguity difference it is one more reason the equivalence-bound framing is better than the p-value.

**Latency (Table X/XI): three runs per arm.** The paper states this plainly and correctly refuses to lean on it ("pinned only to within a factor of four"; "under `always` [the barrier's cost] is not separable from zero at this sample size"; the ≈70× ratio's denominator "pinned only to [27.1, 1524.6] ms"). This is honest, but it means RQ3's deployment-choice table — the paper's most decision-relevant artifact — rests on 3 crash-free runs per configuration because the E5 suspend gate discarded the rest. A reviewer will ask why more crash-free runs (cheap: no fault injection, ~minutes each) were not collected once the gate existed; §VIII says "closing it needs more crash-free runs... and Section VIII says so," which concedes the point without doing the work. This is the cheapest large improvement available to the paper.

### R3.4 Internal validity

Same host, same Redis, author-written provider and author-written baselines: the paper's threats section covers this via (i) the independent SQLite ground-truth oracle with an analysis-source gate that forbids reading live protocol state, (ii) the B4 fairness document, (iii) increments-over-B0 rather than absolute latency, and (iv) §VIII's "detection finding has no referent outside this artifact" — which is the strongest objection this reviewer could construct, stated by the authors against themselves: *nothing external corroborates that an intent record without a barrier suffices for detection, because both systems measured are the authors' own*. The disclosed one-in-432 oracle disagreement (a B4b cell where the provider's ledger recorded 2 of 10 claimed applications, cause undetermined, voided and re-run under a pre-registered-style rule) is handled defensibly, but "we could not determine the cause" of a ground-truth failure is a loose thread: if the oracle can silently under-record, the zeros elsewhere inherit a small unquantified doubt. One sentence bounding that doubt (e.g., the run-log line-count check that caught it runs on every run) would close it.

### R3.5 Is the trilemma a trilemma?

No theorem is offered and the paper does not pretend one is: §I grounds the impossibility of the *fourth* corner (certainty) in FLP/Chandra–Toueg by argument, not proof, and Table IV lists "machine-checked proofs" as a non-claim. What the paper actually has is the standard at-least-once/at-most-once dichotomy — folklore in every messaging text — with the third outcome (terminal, durable, declared unknown) promoted to a first-class, *measured* outcome class. Calling that a "trilemma" is a naming upgrade of a known 2×2, and a grumpy reviewer will say so; but the empirical content (Table VI populating all three columns across seven systems, with only the two intent-bearing systems reaching the third) is real, and Table XII reads as a fair positioning table, not marketing — its own caption concedes "AEP's row is not a stronger guarantee than the others; it is the only one whose residual is declared." The deployment-choice table (XI) is guidance a practitioner can act on *given* the caveat that its numbers rest on three runs; with the current n it is one honest sentence away from marketing, and that sentence is present. Recommendation: consider "three-way trade" (which §I-A already uses) over "trilemma" in the abstract; the weaker word costs nothing and removes the objection.

## R4. Claims vs evidence, sentence level

Overclaims (strength exceeds the referenced number):

1. **§IX: "the analysis pipeline and the raw results are available at https://github.com/...".** False as written. The repository contains 13 frozen *analysis* files; the 432 raw run directories — the actual evidence — are "published as an archive" (`ARTIFACT.md` §5) **with no URL, DOI, or hostname given anywhere in the repository or the paper**. As it stands the raw results are not available at all. See R6. This is the paper's clearest overclaim and the easiest to fix (upload to Zenodo, cite the DOI).
2. **§VI-E: "the voided attempt ships in the artifact under `results/voided/`".** No such path exists in the clone (`experiments/results/` holds `matrix/`, `fsync-always/`, and three `g2-*.json`). Presumably it lives in the unpublished archive, which makes the sentence unverifiable for the same reason as (1).
3. **§VI-B/§VI-C1: the pooled-regime n and p** (Recomputation 2). Not a wrong conclusion; a violated own-rule. The sentence "the weakest of the three comparisons... is significant at p=2.1×10⁻¹⁸³" and "600 executions per arm" should be restated on crashed-only counts (p=1.15×10⁻¹⁸³, n=540), or the reporting rule in §VI-A(e) must be amended to say why the comparison CSV is exempt.
4. **§VI-C1: "one-sided 95% Wilson limit... 0.64%"** (Recomputation 3). Mislabelled quantile; conservative direction; fix the label or the number.
5. **Abstract: "removing the durability barrier... leaves every detection metric unchanged over 600 executions per arm."** "Unchanged" overstates zero-count indistinguishability (the body is more careful: "indistinguishable... bounding each rate"), and 600 is the pooled figure from (3). "Statistically indistinguishable over 540 crashed executions per arm" is both correct and just as strong.
6. **§VI-C2's framing outside its cell.** The text around Table IX is scrupulous, but the abstract's "we give three measured points on that curve" and the introduction's prevention sentences do not carry the one-cell, one-capability, host-latency-dependent scope that §VIII later attaches. A reader of abstract + conclusion alone leaves believing prevention was measured with the same breadth as detection; it was measured at 1/126th of it.

Undersells (the honest review notes both):

7. **The B4→B4b flip is a stronger result than the paper's own framing gives it.** One documented configuration line moves a durable-record engine from 0.53–0.59 duplicates to 0.51–0.54 lost effects, with declared ambiguity pinned at exactly 0.0000 across all 36 cells. That is the crispest empirical statement of the paper's thesis — the existing corners are two silent failure modes and the configuration knob only chooses between them — and it deserves a sentence in the abstract; currently the abstract spends its baseline budget on B0–B2's 0.77–0.83.
8. **The recovery-success rate (0.8333 for AEP/B3 in `comparisons-vs-aep-full.csv`) never appears in the paper's prose** as far as this reviewer can find; if intentional (subsumed by outcome classes), say so, because a reviewer mining the CSVs will find it and wonder.
9. **The E5 timing gate is quietly one of the artifact's best ideas** (wall-vs-monotonic check; a run collected before the gate contributes counts but no timing) and is buried in §VIII(c). Methodology readers would value it promoted to §VI-A.

That is a risk the authors evidently accept, and on balance it earns trust.

## R6. Artifact evaluation (30 minutes, cold)

It succeeded end-to-end on the first attempt — `uv` fetched the pinned Python 3.13.0 automatically, every generated table and `numbers.tex` byte-matched the committed versions, and the state-machine figure matched `aep_core.core.intents`.
