\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

AEP sits at the intersection of three literatures, and its contribution is not
a new mechanism in any of them. Every primitive it uses --- leases, fenced
compare-and-swap, write-ahead logging, an fsync barrier --- is decades old. The
contribution is the \emph{semantics they are composed to deliver}: declared
ambiguity for non-cooperative endpoints, rather than exactly-once for
cooperative ones.

\paragraph{Leases and fencing} Leases are Gray and Cheriton's time-bounded
mutual exclusion under crash faults~\cite{gray1989leases}; Chubby made the
pattern an infrastructural service~\cite{burrows2006chubby}, and the Redis
lock pattern~\cite{redis-locks} is its commodity form. The well-known critique
is that a lease alone is not sufficient, because a lease holder may stall past
expiry and write afterwards; the remedy is a monotonic fencing
token~\cite{kleppmann2016locking}. AEP implements exactly that remedy and adds
the observation that it is not enough for our problem: a fencing token protects
\emph{the store}. Our B1 and B2 baselines make this concrete --- both prevent
state corruption and neither prevents a duplicate external effect
(\cref{tab:outcomes}).

\paragraph{Concurrency control, logging, and long-lived transactions} The
expected-version CAS is optimistic concurrency control~\cite{kung1981optimistic}
applied to a single document. The write-ahead intent is write-ahead logging in
the sense of ARIES~\cite{mohan1992aries} --- record the intention durably
before performing the action --- with the crucial difference that ARIES logs an
action the same system will perform and can therefore undo or redo, while AEP
logs an action a \emph{foreign} system will perform and can do neither. Sagas
address long-lived transactions by pairing each step with a compensating
action~\cite{garciamolina1987sagas}; that is the right answer where a
compensation exists, and AEP's premise is the case where it does not, or where
compensating requires first knowing whether the effect occurred --- which is
the question. Helland's analysis of what survives when distributed transactions
are unavailable~\cite{helland2007apostate}, and his treatment of idempotence as
the property everything else is built on~\cite{helland2012idempotence}, are the
closest conceptual antecedents; AEP can be read as asking what to do when the
idempotence Helland requires cannot be obtained from the far side.

\paragraph{Why certainty is unavailable} That an outcome may be undecidable is
not an engineering shortfall. Consensus is unsolvable in an asynchronous system
with even one crash fault~\cite{fischer1985impossibility}, and what can be
recovered depends on the failure-detection assumptions one is willing to
make~\cite{chandra1996failuredetectors}; replicated state machines buy
agreement between replicas~\cite{ongaro2014raft} but not agreement with a
legacy endpoint that participates in no protocol. AEP takes the impossibility
as given and makes the residual explicit and bounded rather than implicit and
silent.

\paragraph{Durable execution and stateful serverless} This is the closest
active area and the one AEP must be distinguished from most carefully.
Beldi~\cite{zhang2020beldi} provides fault-tolerant, transactional stateful
serverless workflows with exactly-once semantics over its own data
plane. Durable Functions~\cite{burckhardt2021durable} gives a formal semantics
for stateful serverless in terms of replay over a durable history.
Boki~\cite{jia2021boki} builds consistency and fault tolerance on shared logs.
ExoFlow~\cite{zhuang2023exoflow} is the most direct comparison: it targets
exactly-once DAGs and is explicit that the guarantee requires each step to be
recoverable --- idempotent, transactional, or paired with a compensation.

Every one of these systems achieves its guarantee \emph{with the cooperation of
the effectful endpoint}, whether by enrolling it in a transaction, by requiring
idempotence, or by requiring a compensation. Our B4 baseline is an
event-sourced engine of this family, using the same durability barrier as AEP,
and it duplicates --- not because it is badly built, but because it is
correctly implementing at-least-once activity execution against an endpoint
that cannot absorb it. Temporal states the precondition plainly: activities
should be made idempotent, because they may be executed more than
once~\cite{temporal-activity-definition}. B4b shows the documented alternative
--- Maximum Attempts of 1~\cite{temporal-retry} --- purchasing at-most-once and
paying in lost effects. \textbf{The difference AEP claims is not a better
guarantee than these systems provide; it is a different residual.} Where they
must assume the precondition and are silent when it fails, AEP declines to
decide and says so.

\paragraph{Agents} Tool-using and reasoning-acting agents~\cite{yao2023react,
schick2023toolformer} and agent operating systems~\cite{mei2024aios} have made
the workload in this paper common: autonomous, nondeterministic planners
invoking effectful enterprise APIs. The reliability literature for this setting
is young. ACRFence~\cite{zheng2026acrfence} is the nearest work, addressing
semantic rollback in agent checkpoint-restore --- an adjacent hazard from the
same root cause, that an agent's durable record and the world can disagree
after a fault. AEP addresses the forward direction of the same disagreement:
not what a restored checkpoint wrongly believes, but what an interrupted
dispatch cannot determine.
